THE STARTING POINT

What if the dreams we forget every morning became places we could visit?

Dreams are private, unstable and difficult to describe. Their spaces change suddenly, time behaves strangely and most details disappear after waking.

The project explores how those unstable experiences could become a collective exhibition without claiming that every person dreams in the same way.

DESIGN QUESTION

MAKING DREAMS VISITABLE

A dream has no stable architecture. A bedroom can become a station, a familiar person can lose their face and an ordinary corridor can continue forever.

Reconstructing those scenes literally would reduce them to illustrations. Instead, the museum translates their emotional logic into space, light, sound, movement and interaction.

The objective is not to explain dreams. It is to make their instability visitable.

SHARED EMOTIONS · DIFFERENT STORIES

The museum groups dreams by the feeling they leave behind, not only by what happens in them.

Two dreams may contain completely different people, places and events while creating the same feeling of urgency, wonder, nostalgia or fear.

ROOM 01 EVERYDAY

Familiar places becoming subtly wrong.

ROOM 02 CHILDHOOD

Memories rebuilt with distorted proportions.

ROOM 03 ADVENTURE

Wonder, movement and impossible landscapes.

ROOM 04 ROMANCE

Attraction, intimacy and jealousy.

ROOM 05 ACTION

Speed, urgency and loss of control.

ROOM 06 NIGHTMARE

Fear, pursuit and forced awakening.

The rooms represent shared emotional patterns, not one person’s private story.

THE SELECTION PROCESS

HOW DOES A DREAM
ENTER THE MUSEUM?

The museum does not place a submitted dream directly in public view. Every contribution passes through consent, moderation and curatorial review.

01 COLLECT

Dreams are gathered voluntarily through the open collection, interviews and artistic research.

02 PROTECT

Names, precise locations and identifying details are removed.

03 REVIEW

Contributors choose whether their dream remains private, enters the archive or may inspire an artwork.

04 IDENTIFY

Curators identify the main emotion, sensory details and recurring motifs without diagnosing the dreamer.

05 CONNECT

Dreams with related feelings or structures are grouped together.

06 CREATE

Artists transform selected fragments into a new collective installation.

COMPOSITE DREAMS

SEVERAL DREAMS.
ONE INSTALLATION.

Fragments from several anonymous dreams are combined.

One dream may provide the setting, another the movement and another the emotion.

No room claims to reproduce one contributor’s complete private experience.

WHAT MAY BE SELECTED

  • A strong emotional trace
  • A distinctive sensory detail
  • A recurring dream motif
  • An unexpected transformation
  • A connection to other submissions
  • The potential to become a physical experience

Popularity alone does not determine selection. An ordinary dream may reveal a feeling that has not yet appeared elsewhere in the collection.

RESEARCH · NOT INTERPRETATION

SCIENCE PROVIDES CONTEXT.
IT DOES NOT DECODE THE DREAMER.

Each room is informed by peer-reviewed research relevant to its central mechanism. These studies provide context and constraints; they do not assign fixed meanings to symbols, prove one function of dreaming or claim that one finding applies to every person.

ROOM 01 · EVERYDAY DREAMS COMBINE DIFFERENT KINDS OF WAKING MEMORY

Dream reports were linked to recent, remote and mundane waking-life elements. The study does not suggest that dreams replay those experiences faithfully.

VALLAT ET AL. · 2017 ↗ ROOM 02 · CHILDHOOD DREAMS CAN DRAW ON AUTOBIOGRAPHICAL MEMORY

Dream content incorporated autobiographical memories more readily than complete episodic replays. The room translates this as reconstruction, not accurate recovery.

MALINOWSKI & HORTON · 2014 ↗ ROOM 03 · ADVENTURE FALLING AND FLYING ARE COMMON DREAM THEMES

Falling, flying and being chased were among the typical themes reported by many participants. Their frequency does not establish a universal meaning.

SCHREDL ET AL. · 2004 ↗ ROOM 04 · ROMANCE DREAM EMOTIONS WERE ASSOCIATED WITH NEXT-DAY BEHAVIOUR

In a diary study, emotions in dreams involving romantic partners were associated with aspects of relationship behaviour the following day. This does not prove causation.

SELTERMAN ET AL. · 2014 ↗ ROOM 05 · ACTION REM SLEEP NORMALLY SUPPRESSES MOST SKELETAL MOVEMENT

Neural circuits producing REM atonia usually limit the physical enactment of dream activity. Atonia is not absolute and is distinct from the experience of movement.

VETRIVELAN ET AL. · 2023 ↗ ROOM 06 · NIGHTMARE DREAM FEAR ENGAGED BRAIN REGIONS LINKED TO EMOTION

Dream fear was associated with activity in the insula and midcingulate cortex. The finding describes an association, not a fixed interpretation of nightmare imagery.

PROJECT STATUS

SELF-INITIATED
SPECULATIVE
PROJECT.

The Museum of Dreams has not yet been commissioned or produced as a physical exhibition.

The concept, creative direction, narrative system, visitor journey and interactive prototype were developed by Lou-Ann de Sia.

AI-assisted imagery was used as visualisation material for selected environments and characters.

A physical production would require commissioned artists, scientific advisors, accessibility specialists and a complete technical production team.

Dreams disappear quickly. Experiences do not have to.
