THE STARTING POINT

What if the dreams we forget every morning became places we could visit?

Dreams are private, unstable and often impossible to describe. The challenge was to transform this intimate material into a collective experience without pretending that every dream is the same.

HOW CAN SOMETHING INVISIBLE BECOME A PLACE?

Dreams have no stable architecture. Their spaces transform without explanation, time behaves inconsistently and the dreamer often forgets everything within minutes of waking.

The project therefore avoids reconstructing dreams as literal scenes. Instead, it translates their emotional logic into space, movement, sound, light and interaction.

The objective was not to explain dreams. It was to make their instability visitable.

DREAMS ARE CONNECTED THROUGH EMOTIONAL RESONANCE.

No two dreams are identical. However, their emotional structures, sensory details and recurring transformations can echo one another.

HOW DOES A DREAM ENTER THE MUSEUM?

The museum does not reproduce one person’s dream exactly as it was submitted. Dreams are collected, protected, studied and combined before becoming part of an exhibition.

01 · COLLECT

VOLUNTARY SUBMISSION

Dreams are gathered through the open collection, voluntary interviews and international artistic research.

02 · PROTECT

ANONYMISATION

Names, precise locations and identifying details are removed before any material is reviewed.

03 · REVIEW

CONSENT AND MODERATION

Contributors choose whether their dream remains private, enters the anonymous archive or may inspire an artwork. Harmful submissions are rejected.

04 · IDENTIFY

EMOTIONAL MAPPING

Each dream is examined through its dominant feelings, sensory details and recurring motifs—not diagnosed or assigned a universal meaning.

05 · CONNECT

CURATORIAL CLUSTERS

Dreams sharing emotional or structural qualities are connected, even when their stories appear completely different.

06 · CREATE

COMPOSITE DREAMS

Selected fragments are combined into fictional, anonymous sequences that preserve no complete individual account.

SELECTION PRINCIPLES

  • A strong emotional trace
  • A distinctive sensory detail
  • A recurring dream motif
  • An unexpected transformation
  • A connection to other submissions
  • The potential to become a physical experience

Popularity alone does not determine selection. A quiet, ordinary dream may reveal an emotion that has not yet appeared elsewhere.

WHO CREATES THE ROOMS?

01 DREAM CURATOR

Identifies connections and defines the emotional direction of each exhibition.

02 SCIENTIFIC ADVISOR

Reviews the use of sleep research and prevents unsupported interpretation.

03 COMMISSIONED ARTISTS

Translate selected fragments through installation, sculpture, image, music and performance.

04 EXPERIENCE DESIGNER

Connects space, interaction, rhythm and visitor participation.

05 SOUND & LIGHT ARTISTS

Determine what visitors hear, what remains hidden and how the atmosphere responds.

06 PRODUCTION TEAM

Evaluates feasibility, accessibility, safety, technology and production costs.

DREAM SUBMISSION → ANONYMISATION → EMOTIONAL MAPPING → CURATORIAL SELECTION → ARTIST COMMISSION → PHYSICAL INSTALLATION

The artist does not illustrate the dream literally. They translate its emotional structure into something the visitor can see, hear, cross or physically feel.

THE EXPERIENCE FOLLOWS THE STRUCTURE OF ONE NIGHT.

The visitor enters as the sleeper loses consciousness, moves through increasingly unstable environments and leaves when the sleeper wakes.

  1. 01 THE SUNSET

    The outside world becomes quieter and darker.

  2. 02 THE SLEEPER

    The eyes close while neural activity begins.

  3. 03 THE MIND

    Memory, sensation and emotion become exhibition material.

  4. 04 THE GALLERIES

    Each room explores a different emotional dream structure.

  5. 05 THE NIGHTMARE

    The experience loses stability and forces an awakening.

  6. 06 THE ARCHIVE

    The visitor contributes what remains of their own dream.

RESEARCH, NOT INTERPRETATION.

The project does not attempt to decode dreams or assign them universal meanings. Research provides context for the creative direction while uncertainty remains visible.

RESEARCH 01 · MEMORY SOURCES DREAMS RARELY REPLAY MEMORY EXACTLY

In a seven-day protocol, most dream reports were connected to waking-life elements, including recent, remote and mundane experiences. The authors note that dreams rarely replay episodic memories as remembered.

VALLAT ET AL. · PLOS ONE · 2017 ↗ RESEARCH 02 · SPATIAL LEARNING DREAMING OF A TASK WAS ASSOCIATED WITH PERFORMANCE

Participants who dreamed about a virtual navigation task showed greater improvement after sleep. This was an association and does not prove that dreaming alone caused better learning.

WAMSLEY ET AL. · CURRENT BIOLOGY · 2010 ↗ RESEARCH 03 · RELATIONSHIPS SOME DREAM EXPERIENCES ECHOED INTO THE NEXT DAY

Emotions and interactions involving romantic partners in dreams were associated with aspects of relationship behaviour on the following day.

SPECULATIVE,
NOT FICTIONAL CLIENT WORK.

The Museum of Dreams is a self-initiated speculative project. It has not yet been commissioned or produced as a physical exhibition.

The experience, creative direction, narrative system, visitor journey and interactive prototype were developed by Lou-Ann de Sia.

AI-assisted imagery was used as visualisation material for selected environments and characters. The final physical project would require commissioned artists, scientific advisors and a complete production team.

Dreams disappear quickly. Experiences do not have to.
