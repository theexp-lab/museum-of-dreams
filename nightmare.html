RESEARCH 06.A

FEAR · SIMULATED, FELT

Fear reported during dreaming has been associated with activity in regions including the insula and midcingulate cortex.

This describes an observed association, not a universal interpretation of nightmares.

RESEARCH 06.B

THREAT SIMULATION

Threat-simulation theory proposes that some dreams construct virtual dangers and rehearse possible responses.

It remains a scientific hypothesis, not a settled explanation for every nightmare.

PHYSICAL TRANSLATION

HOW THIS BECOMES A MUSEUM ROOM

  • A reconstructed underground platform surrounds the visitor with panoramic projection and directional light.
  • A tracked flashlight reveals a second visual layer containing figures hidden inside the station.
  • Directional speakers move breathing, footsteps and the third heartbeat around the visitor.
  • Low-frequency floor transducers make the pulse physically perceptible.
  • Projection and moving ceiling lights create the sensation of running without moving the visitor through real danger.
  • A sustained-pressure sensor opens the final eye and triggers the transition into white light.
CURATORIAL METHOD

The room translates selected research into a fictional sensory experience.

It does not diagnose the visitor or claim that every nightmare has the same meaning.
